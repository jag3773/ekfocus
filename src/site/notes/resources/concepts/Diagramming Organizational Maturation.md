The Adizes curve is a common framework for showing how organizations mature and then die as they age.

---

![Adizes Curve and Efficiency.jpg](/img/user/archive/attachments/Adizes%20Curve%20and%20Efficiency.jpg)

---

However, in [[resources/zotero/pflaegingOrganizeComplexityHow2014\|Organize for Complexity]], Pflaeging gives another chart which he calls The Beta Choice.

---

![pflaeging-TheBetaChoice.png](/img/user/archive/attachments/pflaeging-TheBetaChoice.png)

---

A bureaucratic end may be the default, but there is an option to maintain agility in the face of complexity.

The [[resources/notes/2026-04-13 Synthesis - Pflaeging and Crabb\|Pflaeging and Crabb synthesis]] deepens this by showing that the Alpha slide has a theological analog: Crabb traces the same arc from naive dependence (Eden / naive Beta) to self-protective independence (the Fall / Alpha bureaucracy). Start-ups slide into Alpha when they misattribute success to products rather than organizational model — just as Adam misattributed the possibility of a superior life to independence from God.

McChrystal's [[resources/zotero/mcchrystalTeamTeamsNew2015\|Team of Teams]] describes the military version of the Beta Choice: replacing command hierarchy with a "shared consciousness" network. Dignan's [[resources/zotero/dignanBraveNewWork2019\|Brave New Work]] offers a practical playbook for organizations attempting the same transition. The [[projects/51.95 S-Curve 2 Meetings\|S-Curve 2 discussions]] at unfoldingWord are exploring whether the organization is pursuing this kind of [[resources/concepts/Theory X and Theory Y\|Theory Y]] renewal or sliding into institutional patterns.
