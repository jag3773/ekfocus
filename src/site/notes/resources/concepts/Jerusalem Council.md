# Jerusalem Council

The Jerusalem Council (c. 49 AD) was the first deliberative assembly of the early church. It settled the question of whether Gentile converts had to be circumcised and follow the Mosaic law to be saved. The council's decision, recorded in [[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:1-29\|Acts 15#15:1-29]] and referenced in [[resources/bible/epistles/Galatians 2#2:1-10\|Galatians 2#2:1-10]], shaped the trajectory of Christianity from a Jewish sect into a global movement.

## The Dispute

The controversy originated in the [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch Church]], where Jewish and Gentile believers had been worshipping together. Men from Judea arrived and began teaching: "Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:1\|Acts 15#15:1]]). This teaching threatened the foundation of Paul's Gentile mission. If accepted, it would require Gentile converts to become Jews before becoming Christians.

[[resources/people/pauline team/Paul\|Paul]] and [[resources/people/pauline team/Barnabas\|Barnabas]] "had no small dissension and debate with them" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:2\|Acts 15#15:2]]). The Antioch church appointed Paul, Barnabas, and others to go to Jerusalem to settle the matter with the apostles and elders.

## The Council

In Jerusalem, "some believers who belonged to the party of the Pharisees" argued that Gentile converts "must be circumcised and required to keep the law of Moses" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:5\|Acts 15#15:5]]). The apostles and elders met to consider the question.

Three speeches shaped the decision:

**Peter** spoke first, recounting how God had given the Holy Spirit to Cornelius and his household (Gentiles) without requiring circumcision: "God, who knows the heart, bore witness to them, by giving them the Holy Spirit just as he did to us, and he made no distinction between us and them, having cleansed their hearts by faith" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:8-9\|Acts 15#15:8-9]]). Peter then posed the decisive question: "Why are you putting God to the test by placing a yoke on the neck of the disciples that neither our fathers nor we have been able to bear?" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:10\|Acts 15#15:10]]).

**Barnabas and Paul** then reported "the signs and wonders God had done through them among the Gentiles" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:12\|Acts 15#15:12]]), providing empirical evidence that God was at work among uncircumcised believers.

**James**, the brother of Jesus and leader of the Jerusalem church, rendered the council's judgment. He grounded the decision in Scripture, citing [[resources/bible/prophets/Amos 9#9:11-12\|Amos 9#9:11-12]] (LXX) to argue that the inclusion of Gentiles fulfilled Old Testament prophecy: "that the rest of mankind may seek the Lord, and all the Gentiles who are called by my name" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:17\|Acts 15#15:17]]). James concluded: "We should not trouble those of the Gentiles who turn to God" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:19\|Acts 15#15:19]]).

## The Decree

The council issued a letter to the Gentile believers in [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch]], Syria, and Cilicia ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:23-29\|Acts 15#15:23-29]]). The letter contained four prohibitions, asking Gentile believers to abstain from:

1. Food polluted by idols
2. Sexual immorality (*porneia*)
3. Meat from strangled animals
4. Blood

These four items are debated among scholars. Some read them as minimal ethical requirements drawn from the Noahide laws or the "stranger" provisions of Leviticus 17-18. Others see them as practical concessions to enable Jewish-Gentile table fellowship, removing the obstacles that would prevent Jews from eating with Gentile believers. The council addressed the social rupture that the [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church#The Antioch Incident\|Antioch incident]] had exposed.

The letter explicitly repudiated the circumcision party: "Since we have heard that some persons have gone out from us and troubled you with words, unsettling your minds, although we gave them no instructions..." ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:24\|Acts 15#15:24]]).

## The Delegation

The council chose [[resources/people/pauline team/Judas Barsabbas\|Judas Barsabbas]] and [[resources/people/pauline team/Silas\|Silas]], "leading men among the brothers" and prophets ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:22, 32\|Acts 15#15:22, 32]]), to carry the letter to Antioch alongside Paul and Barnabas. This ensured the decision had credibility beyond Paul's own network. Judas and Silas could confirm the letter's contents orally and speak with the authority of the Jerusalem church ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:27\|Acts 15#15:27]]).

## Paul's Account in Galatians

Paul's parallel account in [[resources/bible/epistles/Galatians 2#2:1-10\|Galatians 2#2:1-10]] adds details Luke omits. Paul brought [[resources/people/pauline team/Titus\|Titus]], an uncircumcised Greek, to Jerusalem as a living test case. Paul reports that "not even Titus, who was with me, was compelled to be circumcised" ([[resources/bible/epistles/Galatians 2#2:3\|Galatians 2#2:3]]). The "pillars" of the Jerusalem church, James, Peter (Cephas), and John, "added nothing" to Paul's gospel ([[resources/bible/epistles/Galatians 2#2:6\|Galatians 2#2:6]]) and extended "the right hand of fellowship" to Paul and Barnabas, recognizing their mission to the Gentiles ([[resources/bible/epistles/Galatians 2#2:9\|Galatians 2#2:9]]). The only stipulation: "remember the poor" ([[resources/bible/epistles/Galatians 2#2:10\|Galatians 2#2:10]]).

The relationship between Luke's account in Acts 15 and Paul's in Galatians 2 is debated. Some scholars identify them as the same event described from different perspectives. Others place Galatians 2 earlier, at the famine relief visit of Acts 11:30. The chronology remains contested, but the theological outcome is consistent across both accounts.

## Connected People

Members of [[resources/concepts/The Pauline Team\|The Pauline Team]] with direct involvement in the council:

- [[resources/people/pauline team/Paul\|Paul]], argued for Gentile freedom from the law
- [[resources/people/pauline team/Barnabas\|Barnabas]], co-presented the evidence of God's work among Gentiles
- [[resources/people/pauline team/Titus\|Titus]], accompanied Paul as an uncircumcised test case ([[resources/bible/epistles/Galatians 2#2:1-3\|Galatians 2#2:1-3]])
- [[resources/people/pauline team/Silas\|Silas]], chosen to carry the council's decree to Antioch ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:22\|Acts 15#15:22]])
- [[resources/people/pauline team/Judas Barsabbas\|Judas Barsabbas]], co-carried the decree with Silas ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:22\|Acts 15#15:22]])
- [[resources/people/pauline team/John Mark\|John Mark]], the dispute over Mark between Paul and Barnabas occurred in the council's aftermath ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:36-41\|Acts 15#15:36-41]])

## Significance

The Jerusalem Council established three precedents that shaped the rest of church history:

1. **Salvation by faith alone.** Circumcision and Torah observance were not required for Gentile salvation. Peter's argument, "we believe that we will be saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus, just as they will" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:11\|Acts 15#15:11]]), anticipated the theology Paul developed in Romans and Galatians.
2. **Conciliar decision-making.** The apostles, elders, and the whole church deliberated together ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:22\|Acts 15#15:22]]) and reached a consensus guided by Scripture, experience, and the Holy Spirit ("it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us," [[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:28\|Acts 15#15:28]]). This became the model for later church councils.
3. **Unity in diversity.** The council preserved Jewish-Gentile fellowship by requiring minimal adjustments from Gentile believers rather than imposing full Torah compliance. The church held together across ethnic and cultural lines rather than splitting into separate Jewish and Gentile movements.
