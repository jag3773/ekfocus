## Summary

Stephanas's household was "the firstfruits of Achaia" ([[resources/bible/epistles/1 Corinthians 16#16:15\|1 Corinthians 16#16:15]]), the first converts in the entire province of southern Greece. Paul personally baptized his household ([[resources/bible/epistles/1 Corinthians 1#1:16\|1 Corinthians 1#1:16]]), one of only a few baptisms Paul performed in Corinth.

Paul commends the household of Stephanas because "they have devoted themselves to the service of the saints" ([[resources/bible/epistles/1 Corinthians 16#16:15\|1 Corinthians 16#16:15]]). The verb "devoted" means they saw a need and stepped into it voluntarily. Paul urges the Corinthians to "be subject to such as these, and to every fellow worker and laborer" ([[resources/bible/epistles/1 Corinthians 16#16:16\|1 Corinthians 16#16:16]]), a statement that grants Stephanas's household authority based on their service rather than formal appointment.

Stephanas traveled to [[resources/people/pauline team/Paul\|Paul]] in Ephesus with [[resources/people/pauline team/Fortunatus\|Fortunatus]] and [[resources/people/pauline team/Achaicus\|Achaicus]]. Their coming "refreshed my spirit as well as yours" ([[resources/bible/epistles/1 Corinthians 16#16:17-18\|1 Corinthians 16#16:17-18]]). This delegation likely carried the Corinthians' letter of questions and may have carried 1 Corinthians back to Corinth. Paul asks the Corinthians to "give recognition to such people" ([[resources/bible/epistles/1 Corinthians 16#16:18\|1 Corinthians 16#16:18]]).

Stephanas represents lay leadership that emerged naturally in Pauline churches: wealthy householders whose conversion brought their household into the faith, who devoted their resources to serving the community, and who gained authority through service.
