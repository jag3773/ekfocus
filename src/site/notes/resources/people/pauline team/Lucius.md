# Lucius of Cyrene

## Info
- #coworker
- Home:: Cyrene (North Africa)
- Other Locales:: [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch]], [[Corinthian Church\|Corinthian Church]] (possibly)
- References:: [[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 13#13:1\|Acts 13#13:1]], [[resources/bible/epistles/Romans 16#16:21\|Romans 16#16:21]]

## Notes
- Listed among the prophets and teachers at [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch]]
- Paul calls a Lucius "my kinsman" in Romans 16  -  possibly the same person

## Summary

Lucius appears in two New Testament contexts, though whether they refer to the same individual is debated.

In [[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 13#13:1\|Acts 13#13:1]], a "Lucius of Cyrene" is listed among the prophets and teachers in the [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch Church]]. The leadership team included [[resources/people/pauline team/Barnabas\|Barnabas]], [[resources/people/pauline team/Simeon who was called Niger\|Simeon who was called Niger]], [[resources/people/pauline team/Manaen\|Manaen]], and [[resources/people/pauline team/Paul\|Paul]] (still called Saul). Lucius was one of the founding leaders of the [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch Church]], the community that became the base of operations for the Gentile mission. Cyrene was a major city in North Africa (modern Libya) with a significant Jewish population. Cyrenian Jews are mentioned in Acts as among the early evangelists who first preached to Gentiles in [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch]] ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 11#11:20\|Acts 11#11:20]]), so Lucius may have been part of that pioneering effort.

In [[resources/bible/epistles/Romans 16#16:21\|Romans 16#16:21]], Paul sends greetings from "Lucius and [[resources/people/pauline team/Jason\|Jason]] and [[resources/people/pauline team/Sosipater\|Sosipater]], my kinsmen." The term "kinsmen" (*syngeneis*) identifies them as fellow Jews. If this is the same Lucius of Cyrene, he remained an active partner in Paul's mission for over a decade, present with Paul in Corinth during the writing of Romans.

Ancient traditions identified Lucius with Luke the Evangelist, but the identification is incorrect. The names are different in Greek (*Loukios* vs. *Loukas*), and Luke appears to have been a Gentile while Lucius is called a kinsman (fellow Jew). Lucius of Cyrene is his own figure: a North African Jewish-Christian leader who was present at key moments in the expansion of the early church.
