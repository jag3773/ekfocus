## Summary

Judas Barsabbas was a respected leader in the Jerusalem church chosen alongside [[resources/people/pauline team/Silas\|Silas]] to carry the [[resources/concepts/Jerusalem Council\|Jerusalem Council]]'s decree to Gentile believers in [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch]]. Luke identifies both as "leading men among the brothers" (*andras hēgoumenous en tois adelphois*, [[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:22\|Acts 15#15:22]]) and as "prophets" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:32\|Acts 15#15:32]]).

The [[resources/concepts/Jerusalem Council\|Jerusalem Council]] (c. 49 AD) resolved the most explosive controversy of the early church: whether Gentile believers had to be circumcised and follow the Mosaic law. The council's letter addressed "the brothers who are of the Gentiles in [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch]] and Syria and Cilicia" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:23\|Acts 15#15:23]]) and required authorized bearers who could confirm its contents orally: "We have therefore sent Judas and Silas, who themselves will tell you the same things by word of mouth" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:27\|Acts 15#15:27]]). Judas was chosen because his standing in Jerusalem gave the letter credibility.

In [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch]], Judas and Silas "encouraged and strengthened the brothers with many words" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:32\|Acts 15#15:32]]). After spending time there, Judas was "sent off in peace by the brothers to those who had sent them" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 15#15:33\|Acts 15#15:33]]), meaning he returned to Jerusalem, while Silas remained and eventually became [[resources/people/pauline team/Paul\|Paul]]'s partner on the second equipping servant journey.

His patronymic "Barsabbas" ("son of Sabbas/the Sabbath") is shared with Joseph Barsabbas, one of two candidates to replace Judas Iscariot ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 1#1:23\|Acts 1#1:23]]), leading some to speculate they were brothers.
