## Summary

Manaen appears once in [[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 13#13:1\|Acts 13#13:1]], listed among the "prophets and teachers" in the [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch Church]]: "[[resources/people/pauline team/Barnabas\|Barnabas]], [[resources/people/pauline team/Simeon who was called Niger\|Simeon who was called Niger]], [[Lucius of Cyrene\|Lucius of Cyrene]], Manaen a member of the court of Herod the tetrarch, and Saul [[[Paul\|[Paul]]]."

The Greek word *syntrophos*, translated as "member of the court" or "companion", literally means "brought up with" or "foster brother." Manaen was raised alongside Herod Antipas, the ruler who executed John the Baptist ([[resources/bible/gospels/Mark 6#6:17-29\|Mark 6#6:17-29]]) and participated in Jesus's trial ([[resources/bible/gospels/Luke 23#23:8-12\|Luke 23#23:8-12]]). A man reared in the household of one of Israel's most corrupt rulers became a prophet and teacher in the church that launched the Gentile mission.

Manaen's name is the Greek form of the Hebrew Menahem ("comforter"), attested in the Old Testament for a king of Israel ([[resources/bible/prophets/2 Kings 15#15:14\|2 Kings 15#15:14]]). His presence in the [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch]] leadership team, alongside a Cypriot Levite (Barnabas), a North African Jew (Lucius of Cyrene), a man with a Latin nickname (Simeon Niger), and a Pharisee from Tarsus (Paul), illustrates the remarkable social diversity of the [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch Church]]. A court insider, an African, a diaspora intellectual, and a former persecutor served together as leadership of a single congregation.

Manaen was present when the Holy Spirit said, "Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them" ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 13#13:2\|Acts 13#13:2]]). He fasted, prayed, and laid hands on the first equipping servant team, a participant in the moment that launched the organized Gentile mission.
