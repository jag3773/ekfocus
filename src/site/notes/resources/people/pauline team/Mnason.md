# Mnason

## Summary

Mnason appears once in [[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 21#21:16\|Acts 21#21:16]], where Luke records that "some of the disciples from Caesarea went with us, bringing us to the house of Mnason of Cyprus, an early disciple (*archaiō mathētē*), with whom we were to lodge."

The designation "early disciple" (*archaios*) places Mnason among the founding generation of Christians, possibly a believer since Pentecost or shortly after. His Cypriot origin connects him to the Cypriot Jewish community that played an outsized role in early Christianity. [[resources/people/pauline team/Barnabas\|Barnabas]] was also a Cypriot Levite ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 4#4:36\|Acts 4#4:36]]), and "men of Cyprus and Cyrene" were the first to preach the gospel to Gentiles in [[resources/concepts/Antioch Church\|Antioch]] ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 11#11:20\|Acts 11#11:20]]).

By Paul's final visit to Jerusalem, Mnason was living in or near Jerusalem (or between Caesarea and Jerusalem, the text is ambiguous) and hosted Paul's party. This hospitality was significant: Paul was a controversial figure in Jerusalem, suspected by Jewish Christians of teaching Jews to abandon the law ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 21#21:21\|Acts 21#21:21]]). Hosting Paul required both means and willingness to associate with a polarizing apostle.

Mnason bridged two eras of the church. He was among the original disciples and remained active and hospitable when the Pauline mission reached its final stage. His Cypriot origins connect him to the diaspora Jewish network that drove Christianity's earliest expansion.
