## Summary

Sosipater is mentioned once in [[resources/bible/epistles/Romans 16#16:21\|Romans 16#16:21]], where [[resources/people/pauline team/Paul\|Paul]] sends greetings from "Timothy, my fellow worker, and Lucius and [[resources/people/pauline team/Jason\|Jason]] and Sosipater, my kinsmen." The designation "kinsman" (*syngenēs*) identifies Sosipater as a fellow Jew.

The name Sosipater (*Sōsipatros*, "saving father") is the full form of which [[resources/people/pauline team/Sopater\|Sopater]] (*Sōpatros*) is the shortened version. Scholars identify Sosipater with the "Sopater the Berean, son of Pyrrhus" who accompanied Paul on the collection journey to Jerusalem ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 20#20:4\|Acts 20#20:4]]). Sosipater was a Jewish believer from Berea who was present with Paul in Corinth during the writing of Romans and subsequently traveled with Paul to Jerusalem as part of the collection delegation.

The Berean connection is significant. The Berean synagogue community was praised by Luke for their practice of examining Scripture daily ([[resources/bible/gospels/Acts 17#17:11\|Acts 17#17:11]]). If Sosipater came from this community, he was formed in an environment that valued careful scriptural engagement, the kind of person Paul would value as a coworker.

Sosipater's presence alongside [[resources/people/pauline team/Timothy\|Timothy]], [[resources/people/pauline team/Lucius\|Lucius]], and [[resources/people/pauline team/Jason\|Jason]] in Corinth at the time Romans was written places him in Paul's inner circle during one of the most theologically productive moments of Paul's career.
