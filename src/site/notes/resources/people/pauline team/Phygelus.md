# Phygelus

## Info
- #opponent
- Home:: Province of Asia (likely [[Ephesian Church\|Ephesian Church]])
- Other Locales:: None recorded
- References:: [[resources/bible/epistles/2 Timothy 1#1:15\|2 Timothy 1#1:15]]

## Notes
- Named alongside [[resources/people/pauline team/Hermogenes\|Hermogenes]] as one who "turned away" from [[resources/people/pauline team/Paul\|Paul]]

## Summary

Phygelus is mentioned once in [[resources/bible/epistles/2 Timothy 1#1:15\|2 Timothy 1#1:15]], where [[resources/people/pauline team/Paul\|Paul]] writes: "You are aware that all who are in Asia turned away from me, among whom are Phygelus and Hermogenes."

The context is Paul's final imprisonment. The "turning away" (*apestraphēsan*) likely refers to abandoning Paul when he most needed support, refusing to stand with him during trial or distancing themselves from association with a condemned prisoner. Paul names Phygelus and [[resources/people/pauline team/Hermogenes\|Hermogenes]] as representative examples of desertion among Asian Christians.

This abandonment forms the dark backdrop for [[resources/people/pauline team/Onesiphorus\|Onesiphorus]]'s loyalty. Immediately after naming Phygelus and Hermogenes, Paul commends Onesiphorus for "not being ashamed of my chains" and for searching out Paul in Rome ([[resources/bible/epistles/2 Timothy 1#1:16-17\|2 Timothy 1#1:16-17]]). Phygelus chose self-preservation; Onesiphorus chose costly faithfulness.

His name, Phygelus (*Phygelos*), may derive from the Greek root *phygē* ("flight"), which some ancient commentators noted as apt for a man who fled from Paul. Whether this reflects etymological coincidence or intentional irony remains unclear.
