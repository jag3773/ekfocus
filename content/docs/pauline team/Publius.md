## Summary

Publius appears in [[Acts 28#28:7-10|Acts 28:7-10]], during [[Paul]]'s shipwreck and three-month stay on Malta. Luke identifies him as "the chief man of the island" (*ho prōtos tēs nēsou*), a title confirmed by Maltese inscriptions as the official designation for the Roman governor or senior magistrate of Malta.

Publius "received us and entertained us hospitably for three days" ([[Acts 28#28:7|Acts 28:7]]), a generous act given that Paul's party included 276 shipwreck survivors ([[Acts 27#27:37|Acts 27:37]]). During this stay, Paul healed Publius's father, who was "sick with fever and dysentery," by praying and laying hands on him ([[Acts 28#28:8|Acts 28:8]]). News of this healing drew others on the island who were ill, and they too were cured ([[Acts 28#28:9|Acts 28:9]]). In response, the Maltese "honored us greatly, and when we were about to sail, they put on board whatever we needed" ([[Acts 28#28:10|Acts 28:10]]).

Publius's praenomen (*Publius* was a common first name, not a surname) suggests his full Roman name was longer, but Luke uses the personal name only, perhaps reflecting how the islanders addressed him. His hospitality, combined with his father's healing, likely opened the door for the gospel on Malta. Church tradition names Publius as the first bishop of Malta, and later traditions claim he became bishop of Athens, though these identifications are late and unverifiable.
