Task-job conflation is the error of treating "AI can do this narrow, well-defined task" as equivalent to "AI can do this job," when a job is usually a bundle of many tasks, only some of which are the kind a benchmark isolates and measures. A model can score at or above human level on the measured task while the job survives largely intact, because the rest of the job — ambiguous inputs, exception-handling, judgment calls, oversight, the work of deciding what the task even was — is still being done by a person. The conflation runs in both directions: optimists use a narrow-task win to declare a job automated, and alarmists use the same narrow-task win to declare it eliminated, when the real question is what share of the job the measured task actually was and who is still doing the rest.
