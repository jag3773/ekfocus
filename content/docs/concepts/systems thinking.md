A way of understanding reality that emphasizes relationships, patterns, and wholes rather than isolated parts. In systems thinking, you study how components interact to produce emergent behavior that cannot be predicted from the components alone. A system's performance lives in the connections between parts, not in the parts themselves.

See [Wikipedia: Systems thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_thinking) and [[meadowsThinkingSystemsInternational2008|Thinking in Systems by Donella Meadows]].

## Core Principles

Meadows identified several key patterns: feedback loops (reinforcing and balancing), delays (the gap between action and consequence), leverage points (places where small changes produce large effects), and the counterintuitive behavior of complex systems (the obvious solution often makes things worse).

Pflaeging applies systems thinking to organizations in [[pflaegingOrganizeComplexityHow2014|Organize for Complexity]]. His "improvement paradox" is pure systems thinking: working on individual components damages the whole, because the system's performance lives in the interactions ([[2026-04-13 Summary - Organize for Complexity|Pflaeging summary]]). Toyota's "5 Whys" technique resists the temptation to fix symptoms, instead tracing causes through the system to their root.
