# Missionary Methods - Summary

Roland Allen's [[allenMissionaryMethodsSt2011|Missionary Methods]] (1912) asks a single disarming question of every mission society of his day: are we using St. Paul's methods or our own? Writing as an Anglican missionary with years in China behind him, Allen argues that Paul planted churches that were self-supporting, self-governing, and self-propagating within months, while modern missions produce dependent congregations that need decades of foreign subsidy and supervision. The gap, he insists, is not explained by any advantage in Paul's world, so it must lie in method, and the fault is ours.

The book reads Acts as instruction rather than legend. Allen begins by [[Allen Reads Acts as a Manual Not as the Exceptional Exploits of One Man|treating Luke's account as a reproducible manual]] and then diagnoses why the West cannot follow it: [[Allen Says the Western Spirit Cannot Stop Itself From Exporting Its Own Machinery|the restless Western temperament confuses the gospel with its own organizational machinery]] and exports both. From there he works through Paul's practice point by point. Paul chose centres that [[Allen's Strategic Centres Were Strategic Because He Made Them Seedbeds of Self-Propagation|became strategic only because the churches inside them could reproduce]]. He kept money and mission rigorously apart, following [[Paul's Three Money Rules Left Every Church to Fund and Govern Itself|three financial rules that left every church to fund and govern itself]], because [[Imported Buildings and Subsidy Pauperize Converts Into Passive Recipients|imported buildings and subsidy pauperize converts into passive recipients]] and build the foreign dependence the mission should most want to destroy. He refused to buy belief, holding that [[Allen Says the Gospel Must Never Be Bought With Charity or Healing|the gospel must never be purchased with charity or healing]]. And he [[Paul Left Behind Equipped Churches Not Dependent Congregations|left behind fully equipped churches rather than dependent congregations]], ordaining local ministers for local people and then moving on.

Inside the vault Missionary Methods is the 1912 predecessor of Allen's [[The Spontaneous Expansion of the Church - Summary|later and fuller argument]], and the two books share one engine: a healthy church spreads on its own, so [[Spontaneous Expansion Is the Natural Activity of Healthy Christian Life|spontaneous expansion is the natural activity of healthy Christian life]]. Allen is the author who gave the [[Three-Self Church|three-self framework]] its most influential articulation, which makes this book a primary root for [[Church Planting Movement]] missiology and a direct rebuke to [[managerial missiology]]. His refusal of subsidy collides with the patron model the vault celebrates elsewhere, traced in [[Tension - Allen Refuses the Standing Subsidy That Gospel Patrons Celebrates|the tension with Gospel Patrons]], and his refusal of mission control reopens the structural disagreement in [[Tension - Allen Says Organization Suppresses Expansion While Johnson Says No Movement Survives Without It|the Allen-versus-Johnson tension]].
