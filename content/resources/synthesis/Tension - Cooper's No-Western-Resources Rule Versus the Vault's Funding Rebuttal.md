Cooper and the vault's funding rebuttal pull in opposite directions on outside money, and the disagreement is worth holding open rather than resolving too quickly.

Cooper argues in [[cooperEphesiologyStudyEphesian2020|Ephesiology]] that a church plant should aim from the start to "grow exponentially over time, without relying on Western resources," and that the five-self model "prevents missionaries from setting up structures that cannot be sustained by the resources of the new church." Outside subsidy, on this view, is a threat to indigeneity. A self-financing church is the goal, and dependence on foreign money is the failure state to design against.

The [[2026-05-12 Rebuttal to NT Funding Taxonomy|funding rebuttal]] presses two objections that bear directly on this. First, it documents that Paul ran organized, supra-local fundraising: he "raised it across at least four provinces," "appointed named delegates from multiple cities to manage it," and "built and managed a centralized funding operation" through the Jerusalem collection. If the apostolic record itself shows cross-regional money moving toward churches in need, then a flat no-outside-resources rule cannot claim to be simply the New Testament pattern. Second, and more fundamentally, the rebuttal challenges the very move Cooper makes, warning that "the normative claim that first-century funding patterns should govern modern practice rests on a theological commitment" rather than a demonstrated one, and citing Andrew Walls on how the gospel takes different institutional forms across cultures.

The tension is not whether indigeneity is good. Both sides prize local ownership. It is whether self-financing is a non-negotiable mark of a healthy church or one contextual value that can trade off against generosity, solidarity, and the practical limits a young church faces. Cooper treats financial independence as definitional. The rebuttal treats it as a variable, and reads the collection as evidence that the early church itself moved resources across regions when love required it.
